Case study

HaysMac LinkedIn video content

Turning expert conversations into a bank of useful, LinkedIn-first video content for HaysMac.

Across multiple filmed conversations, HaysMac has used a repeatable format: capture one strong discussion, then shape it into full-length thought-leadership videos, hero edits and shorter social clips.

Project overview

HaysMac wanted a practical way to turn expert-led conversations into a steady stream of professional video content for LinkedIn and wider marketing.

The approach stays consistent from project to project: film one natural conversation, keep the production polished but unobtrusive, then edit the footage into multiple pieces with different jobs to do.

The latest example is a conversation between a HaysMac partner and Marcus Stuttard, Head of AIM at the London Stock Exchange. From that single conversation came a full-length film, three hero edits and a wider set of social-first clips designed for the feed.

Latest example

A conversation designed to become multiple assets

The AIM conversation was filmed in HaysMac's office with a lean interview setup, clean audio and lighting that kept the look polished without making the discussion feel overproduced.

That long-form conversation became the source material for several layers of content: a complete discussion, longer thematic edits and shorter social clips built to hold attention quickly in the feed.

It shows how one well-run filming session can create a more useful content bank than a single one-off video.

Built for LinkedIn

Short social edits from the AIM conversation

The full discussion was repurposed into shorter feed-first edits with 4:5 framing, strong opening hooks, burned-in subtitles, speaker name and title branding, consistent HaysMac branding and self-contained ideas drawn from the wider conversation.

Why this works for professional services

Professional services firms already have people with valuable knowledge, opinions and experience. A well-run filmed conversation gives that expertise a natural format that feels human rather than over-scripted.

From there, one discussion can become a consistent stream of useful content: a full conversation for depth, standalone edits for key themes, and shorter LinkedIn clips for focused ideas that work quickly in the feed.

The HaysMac projects show the same model working more than once, while allowing the format itself to evolve as the social content becomes more intentional.
